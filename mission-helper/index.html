Mission board optimiser

1. How the board actually works

You get 3 missions a day from a board of 6, plus one free refresh.

Every mission pays out 4 reward slots. Each slot independently rolls one of the six resources at 1 in 6 odds, so a mission can roll four of the same thing or four different things.

Why the amounts are not the interesting part

The amount is not random. This is the part people get wrong. Each resource has a fixed base amount set by the mission's level — a goo slot on a given mission always pays the same number. So a mission is never "a big one" or "a small one": the only thing that varies is which six resources its four slots land on.

Mission level comes from your Hammer Thief level, which unlocks a bracket of levels the board can roll from. Level moves the base amounts by roughly 1% per level, and your bracket only spans 6–10 levels, so level is worth a few percent at most. The reward mix is what matters, by a mile. Goo is the one exception worth knowing about: it is a small stepped integer rather than a smooth curve, so it can jump from 6 to 7 to 8 inside a single bracket.

Because the board can roll any level in your bracket, the per-slot figures below are averages across that range. The range is shown next to each one.

Assumptions and sources
  • Base amounts and level brackets read from MissionRewardLibrary.json; level brackets cross-checked against the in-game Refresh Info table; board size, slot count and daily energy from MissionBaseConfig.json.
  • Each of the four slots is assumed to be an independent, equally likely roll across the six resources. The refresh is assumed free and to replace all six missions without costing you a run.
  • Mission levels are assumed uniform across your bracket. Board-to-board level differences are ignored in the strategy because they are worth ~1% per level — if two missions have identical rewards, take the higher level one.
  • Daily figures come from 80,000 simulated days playing the strategy above.
  • Per-slot amounts are the mean across every mission level in your bracket, so they are not values that appear verbatim in the config — the range column shows what a single mission can actually pay.
  • The goo bonus scales what you collect, not the bars. Rate goo higher if you want the strategy itself to chase it harder.
  • Hammer Thief level converts to the in-game stage label as world = floor((level−1)/10)+1, stage = ((level−1) mod 10)+1, so level 100 is stage 10-10.
